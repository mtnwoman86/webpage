Science

Ulyana's work spans glaciology, climate change, pollution, planetary science, and science communication. Her research has taken her from the Himalaya to the Arctic and Antarctic, while her writing focuses on making science accessible to audiences beyond academia.

Current Research

Persistent Pollutants in the Cryosphere

Ulyana's current research investigates the presence and movement of human-made pollutants in some of Earth's most remote environments. Her work focuses on black carbon, microplastics, PFAS ("forever chemicals"), and other light-absorbing particles in snow, glacier ice, sea ice, and seawater.

Her recent fieldwork has included sampling across the Arctic Ocean from Svalbard to Alaska, as well as research in Antarctica, Greenland, the Canadian Arctic, Iceland, and high-elevation mountain environments including the Himalayas, Alps and Andes.

In 2025, NASA funded Ulyana's Persistent Pollutants project, which combined Arctic field research with community and student participation in Shishmaref, Alaska. The project integrates measurements of microplastics and light-absorbing particles with NASA Earth observations and educational programs connecting Arctic communities with ongoing environmental research.

Glaciers & The Changing Climate

Ulyana's research began in the Himalaya, where she studied the evolution of supraglacial lakes on debris-covered glaciers and the hazards associated with glacial lake development and flooding. Her Ph.D. research at the University of Colorado Boulder combined field observations, instrumentation, satellite remote sensing, and repeat photography to document rapidly changing glacier systems in the Mount Everest region.

Her work continues to examine changes in the cryosphere and the ways field observations, historical imagery, and modern satellite data can reveal environmental change over timescales ranging from years to more than a century.

Scientific American Blog Writing

Ulyana has written firsthand accounts of field science for Scientific American, bringing readers along on expeditions in the Mount Everest region of Nepal.

Her 2014 dispatches documented the realities of conducting high-altitude glacier research, including her search for black carbon deposited on Himalayan snow and ice. She also wrote from Nepal during the deadly 2014 Mount Everest avalanche, providing an unusual perspective at the intersection of an unfolding mountaineering tragedy and scientific fieldwork.
